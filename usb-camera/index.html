OTG connection & troubleshooting

No picture?
Start with these three checks.

If your USB endoscope or capture card will not connect on Android 14 or 15, check the connection before changing the camera.

  1. 01

    Enable USB OTG

    Use a phone that supports USB Host / OTG. On some OPPO, Vivo and OnePlus phones, search Settings for “OTG” or “OTG connection” and turn it on before reconnecting.

    Some models switch OTG off after inactivity. Menu names vary by phone and OS.
  2. 02

    Allow the USB connection

    Open USB Camera, connect your device and allow access in Android’s USB permission dialog. Allow the camera permission if requested. If you dismissed the prompt, unplug and reconnect.

    Close other camera apps if the channel is busy. A missing picture does not always mean permission was denied.
  3. 03

    Check the cable & power

    Use a data-capable OTG adapter, not a charging-only cable. LED endoscopes and capture cards may need a compatible powered OTG hub. Try a short cable and reduce extra accessories.

    If the device is detected but stays black, try a lower video mode that the camera actually supports.

Ready to reconnect? Open the app’s connection guide and check your device’s reported modes.

Why does my USB endoscope show a black screen on Android 14 or 15?

Check that your phone supports USB Host / OTG, enable OTG if your phone has a switch, then reconnect and allow the USB and camera permissions requested by Android. Close other camera apps. Use a data-capable adapter and try a powered OTG hub if the camera needs more power. If the device connects but no picture appears, try a lower resolution that the camera actually reports. A Wi-Fi-only or proprietary camera may need its manufacturer’s app.

Does USB Camera require root access?

No. USB Camera uses Android’s USB device permission flow. You need a phone or tablet with USB Host / OTG support and a compatible UVC camera. Grant the permissions requested by Android; rooting the phone is not part of setup.

Can I use my phone as a portable HDMI monitor?

Yes, with a compatible USB HDMI capture card that outputs UVC video. Connect the HDMI source to the capture card, then connect the card to your phone through a suitable OTG adapter. A USB-C to HDMI display-output cable cannot capture video. Resolution and latency depend on the source, capture card and phone; HDCP-protected sources may not produce a picture.

Which video formats and resolutions are supported?

USB Camera works with compatible MJPEG and YUYV / YUY2 UVC video modes and saves recordings as MP4. Available resolutions and frame rates come from the connected device. 1080p or 4K requires a matching camera output mode, enough USB bandwidth and a capable phone. H.264-only USB input is not guaranteed; choose MJPEG or YUYV when available. Digital zoom enlarges the image without adding optical detail.
