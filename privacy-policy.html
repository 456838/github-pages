2. How we collect and process your data (Local-First Design & Data Categories)

Most NewSalton applications are engineered with a local-first architecture and do not require you to register an account or log in. Core functional data—such as your live USB/UVC camera video and audio feeds, captured photos, recorded MP4 videos, local streaming configurations, installed application lists, exported APK backup files, and device control sessions—is processed directly on your local device and is never uploaded to NewSalton servers unless you explicitly export, share, or submit a diagnostic report.

 

We may collect or process limited information in the following circumstances:

    • when you download, launch, and use our mobile or desktop apps;
    • when you grant operating system permissions to use hardware or storage features;
    • when you purchase a Pro subscription or one-time upgrade through an app store;
    • when you voluntarily submit an in-app hardware compatibility or diagnostic error report; or
    • when you otherwise communicate with us via email or support channels.

 

When you access our websites and our apps, we do not intentionally collect legally sensitive personal data (such as race, ethnicity, religious or philosophical beliefs, trade union membership, genetic data, biometric identification templates, health records, sexual orientation, or criminal history) or sensitive financial credentials (such as social security numbers, bank account numbers, or raw credit/debit card numbers).

 

Depending on the specific application and features you use, the data processed may include:

    • Device Permissions and Local Hardware Data (Processed Locally): Depending on the app's purpose, the app may request OS permissions such as Camera permission (required by Android for UVC external camera access and video preview), Microphone permission (for audio recording alongside video), USB Host / OTG device access (to communicate with connected USB cameras, endoscopes, or capture cards), Storage / Photos & Media access (to save or export photos, videos, or APK backups to your device), Query All Packages / App Usage permission (in app uninstaller/manager utilities, solely to display and manage installed apps locally on your device), and Local Network / Wi-Fi / Foreground Service permissions (for local browser preview, ONVIF/RTMP/SRT streaming, or uninterrupted background recording). All such permission data is used strictly to perform the user-initiated feature on your device.
    • Technical, Diagnostic, and Crash Information: Technical metadata such as IP address, mobile device model, CPU/ABI architecture, operating system version, app version and build number, language and time zone settings, software crash stack traces, ANR (Application Not Responding) traces, and USB hardware vendor/product descriptor IDs (VID/PID) when diagnosing camera connection failures.
    • In-App Usage and Interaction Telemetry: Anonymized or pseudonymized app interaction events (such as screen views, feature activation counts, preview startup latency, and error codes) used to identify bugs, optimize cold-start and rendering performance, and improve user experience.
    • Purchase and Entitlement Status: When you make an in-app purchase or subscribe via Google Play Billing or Apple StoreKit, the platform processes your payment and provides us with non-sensitive transaction metadata (such as product ID, subscription status, trial usage state, purchase token, and order ID) so the app can unlock Pro features.
    • Advertising Identifiers (In Ad-Supported Apps Only): In free or ad-supported applications (such as apps integrating Google AdMob), third-party advertising SDKs may process device advertising identifiers (such as Google Advertising ID (AAID) or Apple IDFA, subject to your OS permission), coarse location derived from IP address, and ad impression/click metrics.

We may employ reputable third-party platform services—specifically Google Play Services, Google Firebase (Firebase Analytics and Firebase Crashlytics), Google AdMob (in ad-supported apps), and Apple StoreKit—that use standard SDK methods to collect technical telemetry necessary for crash reporting, analytics, billing verification, and ad delivery.
